What each idea is worth

Every search feature in the engine has an off switch. That's how I tested them: same position, same depth, one feature removed, count the work. A good optimization reaches the same answer with fewer nodes.

How it got here

I started because I wanted to know what actually happens inside a chess engine. No chess library: the board, the rules, the search, and the hashing are all mine.

Positions live in 64-bit bitboards, one integer per piece type. Every move is reversible, so the search makes and unmakes moves instead of copying the board. Positions are hashed incrementally with Zobrist keys covering pieces, side to move, castling rights, and en passant.

The evaluation counts material (pawn 100, knight 320, bishop 330, rook 500, queen 900), adds piece-square tables that blend from middlegame to endgame as pieces come off, and a mop-up term that herds a lone king to the edge when it's winning.

The lesson that stuck: an algorithm that should be faster on paper isn't always stronger on a clock. So each step was measured before it stayed.
